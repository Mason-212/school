> > [!left]
> > ### <u>**Habitat**</u>
> >
> > A habitat is all the biotic and abiotic components in an environment where an organism lives, including things such as the food the organism eats, the dens it uses, and the air temperature.
> >
> > - Many species live in the same habitat
>
> > [!right]
> > ### <u>**Niche**</u>
> >
> > An ecological niche contains all the physical, chemical, and biological factors a species needs to survive and reproduce including:
> >
> > - **Food Source:** Type of food a species eats, competes with others for food, and where it fits in a food web

**Key Idea:** Habitat is where they live; Niche is how they live and the role they have.

> [!columns3]
>
> > [!left]
> > ### <u>**Population Density**</u>
> >
> > Population Density = <u>total Population</u> / <u>total area (km²)</u>
> >
> > Population density refers to the number of individuals of a specific species living within a given unit of area — essentially measuring how crowded a population is within a certain habitat.
>
> > [!mid]
> > ### <u>**Growth Patterns**</u>
> >
> > #### <u>**Exponential Growth**</u>
> >
> > Occurs when a population size increases dramatically over a relatively short amount of time. Looks like a J-shaped curve.
>
> > [!right]
> > #### <u>**Logistic Growth**</u>
> >
> > Begins with a period of slow growth followed by a time of rapid growth, then plateaus into an S-shaped curve.

### <u>**Carrying Capacity**</u>


A species average population size in a particular habitat. The species' size is limited by environmental factors like adequate food, water, shelter, and mates. Carrying capacity can change based on environmental factors.

**Density Dependent limiting factors:** Factors that depend on population size (competition, predation, food, disease)

**Density Independent limiting factors:** Affects all populations in a similar way independent of population size

Drought, wildfire, volcanic eruptions, hurricanes, and deforestation.
