## 1. Homeostasis vs reacting to stimuli

**Question:** Your notes define homeostasis as living things regulating internal conditions within a range that supports life. In plain terms, how is that different from simply “reacting to stimuli”?

**Your answer:** Reacting to stimuli has to do with outside threats while homeostasis only focuses on the internal organs; reacting to stimuli usually has a broader range of actions.

**Correct answer:** Reacting to stimuli = detect and react to changes in the environment (not only threats). Homeostasis = regulate internal conditions (temperature, water balance, etc.) within a range that supports life — not “internal organs.” A stimulus response can help homeostasis, but the definitions target different things.

**How to do it:** Pull the two definitions side by side. Stimuli → environment change → detect/react. Homeostasis → keep the inside in a livable range. Don’t shrink stimuli to “threats” or homeostasis to “organs.”

**Where you went wrong:** Narrowed stimuli to threats and framed homeostasis as organs instead of regulated internal conditions.

## 2. Organization chain — organism → population (and what comes after)

**Question:** In `cell → … → organism → population → community → ecosystem → …`, where does the shift from “parts of one living thing” to “many individuals / groups in nature” happen, and what changes about what you’re studying at that jump?

**Your answer:** Organism → population (scale gets larger). Then said many individuals → groups happens at community → ecosystem because of consumers and producers.

**Correct answer:** Main jump is **organism → population** (one individual → many of the same species). After that: population → community adds different species interacting; community → ecosystem adds abiotic factors — not “consumers and producers” as the defining change.

**How to do it:** Find where “one organism” ends. Next level = population. Then check each step: same species → many species → plus nonliving.

**Where you went wrong:** Got the first jump, but mislabeled community → ecosystem as the “groups” shift and tied it to consumers/producers instead of abiotic.

## 3. Metabolism definition

**Question:** What does metabolism mean in your Characteristics of Life notes, in your own words?

**Your answer:** The cycle of an organism consuming food and getting the energy it needs to live from that food.

**Correct answer:** Living things obtain and use **energy and matter** to carry out life processes — not only eating food (plants use light + matter too).

**How to do it:** Quote the notes: obtain/use energy **and** matter. Don’t limit it to animals eating.

**Where you went wrong:** Shrunk metabolism to “consume food for energy” and left out matter / non-food energy paths.

## 4. Biosphere vs ecosystem

**Question:** What is the biosphere in the four-spheres notes, and how is it different from an ecosystem in the organization levels?

**Your answer:** Biosphere is more about states of matter; ecosystem is entities from different biospheres and their interactions.

**Correct answer:** Biosphere = living organisms on Earth (microbes, fungi, plants/animals). Ecosystem = organisms interacting with each other and the nonliving environment in an area (community + abiotic). Ecosystem is local biotic+abiotic; biosphere is Earth-wide living sphere / top of the chain.

**How to do it:** Biosphere → life on Earth. Ecosystem → community + abiotic in a place. Don’t mix biosphere with states of matter (hydrosphere water phases).

**Where you went wrong:** Confused biosphere with states of matter and treated ecosystems as mixing “different biospheres.”
