## Athens Greece (Athenian Democracy)

In ancient Greece, several principles were established that became crucial (important) to the shaping of Western civilization and its political values. These were ideas such as the worth and dignity of the individual, the value of reason in decision-making, the concept of citizenship, and the idea of political freedom.

The ideas of political freedom were best expressed by the Greek city-state of Athens. During its “golden age” in the fifth century B.C., Athens set up a direct democracy. This meant that ALL adult male citizens were members of the legislature. Women were considered citizens, but they could not participate in law-making, nor could slaves or foreign residents. The male citizen of Athens met about 40 times a year to debate and decide public issues. The assembly was also able to: declare war, sign treaties, and spend taxpayer money. <mark style=“background:#fff88f”>The lowest citizen had as much right as the wealthiest to vote, to hold office, and express his opinion in the Assembly.</mark>

Athens has been described as a government of amateurs. There were no professional politicians, judges, or soldiers. Rather the duties of government were performed by ordinary citizens. Each year, public officials were chosen by lottery. They held office for one year, and then could not hold office again. Clearly a system like that was based on the idea that the average citizen was capable of participating intelligently in the affairs of the government.


Pericles a prominent statesman and philosopher of Greece said:

“And just as our political life is free and open, so is our day-to-day life in our relations with each other. We do not get into a state (argument) with our neighbor if he enjoys himself in his own way, nor do we give him the kind or bad looks which, although do no real harm, still do hurt people’s feelings. We are free and tolerant in our private lives, but in public affairs, we keep to the law. This is because the law commands our deep respect.”

# The Role of Reason

The ideals of a democratic state could have arisen only in a society that had an awareness and respect for human intelligence and the power of reason. The Greeks were the first people in the world who tried to make scientific investigations of nature and systematic studies of human culture. Through their brilliant work, they made all future ages aware of the strength and potential of the human mind.

Earlier people including the Greeks of earlier ages, had interpreted the world through myths. They believed that gods and demons controlled everything that happened in the world. The first Greek philosophers were not satisfied with their investigations, they arrived at a new view of nature and a new way of studying it. They said that nature was not controlled by arbitrary and willful gods, but rather that all things in the natural world followed predictable patterns, which they called natural law. The rules of nature could be discovered through careful observation and reason questioning by human beings.

It was a great achievement of the Greeks to question old ways of looking at the world. Over time, every aspect of Greek civilization - science, art, literature, and politics - showed a growing reliance on reason and less dependence on supernatural or traditional explanations. With this achievement, the Greeks broke decisively with the past and established ideals that were essential to the later development of Western Civilization.
