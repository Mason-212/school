## 1. Roman Republic founding year

**Question:** In what year did the Romans set up their republic, and what were the three branches (with their roles) described in the text?

**Your answer:** `450 BC`; branches: executive (two consuls), legislative (Senate), judicial (Praetors)

**Where you went wrong:** Branches were right; the year slipped to 450 instead of 509.

## 2. Stoic natural law (definition)

**Question:** In your own words, what did the Stoics mean by natural law, and why did that idea lead them to say people are basically equal?

**Your answer:** Natural law = everyone is born with skills like reasoning (some better than others) and the same basic rights; equality follows from shared rights and capacity to reason.

**Correct answer:** Natural law = there are rational standards in nature that apply to all people at all times. Stoics: all humans can reason (to different degrees), so all are subject to the same moral laws; society’s laws cannot violate that — which supports natural rights no legitimate government can take away.

**How to do it:** Separate (1) what natural law *is* (universal rational standards) from (2) the Stoic *argument* for equality (shared reason → same moral laws → natural rights).

**Where you went wrong:** Equated natural law with “born with skills/rights” instead of universal rational standards; the equality half was close.

## 3. Athens: “rule by the people” vs. practice

**Question:** Athens called itself a democracy, but women, slaves, and foreign residents could not make laws. What does that reveal about the gap between the *idea* of “rule by the people” and who actually held power in Athenian practice?

**Your answer:** Loose definition of citizen; “rule by the people” only means rule by those allowed power, so a minority held power and Athens’s claim was false.

**Correct answer:** Political power belonged to adult male citizens only (a minority of the population). Women were citizens but barred from law-making; slaves and foreign residents were also excluded. Among adult male citizens, rich and poor were equal in the Assembly. So democracy was real inside a narrow “people,” not a full “rule by all.”

**How to do it:** Split (1) who lived in Athens, (2) who counted as citizens, (3) who could actually make laws. Then judge the slogan against that narrow eligible group.

**Where you went wrong:** Missed that women were citizens yet still excluded from law-making; called the claim flatly “false” instead of limited/incomplete for a narrow group.
