## A verb expresses action or a state of being.
In this lesson, verbs are classified as action or linking verbs, helping or main verbs, and transitive or intransitive verbs.

# Action Verbs & Linking Verbs:
An ***action verb*** expresses either physical or mental activity.
**PHYSICAL:** **travel / sit / arise/ draw/ build**
MENTAL: **remember / think / believe/ consider / know**

**EXAMPLES:**

The ancient Egyptians constructed elaborate tombs for their rulers. [The Verb ***Constructed*** physical activity.]
 Do you recall the family we met on our vaction last summer? **The verb phrase Do recall **Expresses mental activity

A ***linking verb*** connects the subject to a word or word group in the predicate that identifies or describes the subject. Such a word or word group is called a subject complement

**Examples:**

 Patience is the best remedy for many troubles. [The subject complement **remedy** identifies the subject **patience** ]

Commonly used **linking verbs:**
am / be / will be / had been / is / can be / could be / shall have been

**are / may be / should be / will have been / was / might be / would be / could have been / were / must be / has been / should have been / being / shall be / has been / have been / would have been**

Some verbs may be used as ***linking verbs*** in one context and as ***action verbs*** in a different context.

**EXAMPLES:**
LINKING: The soup **tasted** spicy
ACTION: We ***tasted*** the soup
LINKING: She ***flet*** good about her presentations.
ACTION: The explorers ***felt*** rain on their faces.


The forms of the verb ***be*** are not always used as ***linking verbs***. They may be followed by words that tell ***when*** or ***where***. Used in this way, be ***be*** is referred to as a ***state-of-being verb.***

EXAMPLE:
My relatives from Ohio ***will be*** here tomorrow.

# MAIN VERBS & HELPING VERBS:
A verb phrase consists of at least one ***main verb*** and one or more ***helping verbs.***

**EXAMPLES:**
John **will be arriving** at 3:00 PM **Will** and **be** are the helping verbs: **arriving** is the main verb

Commonly used helping verbs:
Forms of Be: am / is / are / was / were / be / being / been
Forms of **Have**: has / have / having / had
Forms of **Do:** do / does / doing / did
Modals: may / might / must / can / shall / will / could / should / would

A **modal** is a helping verb that is joined with a main verb to express an attitude such as necessity or possibility.

EXAMPLES: We ***must*** be on time if we want to catch the plane.[Necessity]

***Helping verbs***   may be seperated from the main verb by other words

***Should*** we ***leave*** immediately?
Should is the helping verb
leave is the main verb

# TRANSITIVE & INTRASITIVE VERBS:
A ***transitive verb*** has an object-a word that tells *who* or *what* receives the action.

EXAMPLES:
Everyone in the school ***cheered*** the football team during the championship game [ The object **team** receives the action of **cheered**]

An **intransitive verb*** does not have an object(AKA, more vague)

EXAMPLES: The gorilla **smiled.**

Some verbs can be ***transitive*** in one context and ***intransitive in a different context.***
