THE PREPOSITION.

Position (for caution verbs)
A preposition shows the relationship of a noun or pronoun, called the object of the preposition, to another word.

Notice how the prepositions in the examples show different relationships between the words ran (the verb) and me (the object of each preposition).

#### <u>**EXAMPLES**</u>


The playful puppy ran <u>beside</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>toward</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>around</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>past</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>after</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>behind</u> me.

The playful puppy ran <u>in front of</u> me.

The playful puppy ran in front of me.

#### <u>**PREPOSITIONAL PHRASES**</u>


A preposition, its object, and any modifiers of the object form a prepositional phrase.

#### <u>**EXAMPLES**</u>


I swam across the raging river. [Across is the preposition. River is the object of the preposition. The and raging are modifiers of the object.]
