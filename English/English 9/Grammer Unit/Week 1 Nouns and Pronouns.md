
### Nouns: 

#### **A noun is a word that stands for a person, place, thing, or idea.**

EXAMPLES:

Persons – architect / travelers / family / Kira Alvarez

Places – restaurant / islands / wilderness / Salt Lake City

Things – computer / sailboats / insects / Brooklyn Bridge

Ideas – education / beliefs / ambition / utopianism

COMMON AND PROPER NOUNS:

Nouns can be categorized in different ways.  One way that we can categorize nouns is by classifying them as either common or proper. 

A common noun names any one of a group of persons, places, things, or ideas.  A proper noun names a particular person, place, thing, or idea.  Generally, common nouns are not capitalized unless they begin a sentence or are part of a title; proper nouns are always capitalized.

EXAMPLES:

Common – student / player / movie / citizens

Proper – Alejandro / Steve Young / The Matrix / Germans

SINGULAR AND PLURAL NOUNS:

Nouns can also be categorized as either singular or plural. 

A singular noun names just one person, place, thing, or idea.  A plural noun names more than one (multiple) person, place, thing, or idea.

EXAMPLES:

Singular – book / fox / person / Moraga

Plural – books / foxes / people / cities

Rules for forming the plural forms of nouns:

1. For most nouns, add s (example: dancer = dancers).

2. For nouns ending in s, x, z, ch, or sh, add es (example: dress = dresses; fox = foxes).

3. For nouns ending in y preceded by a vowel, add s (example: journey = journeys).

4. For nouns ending in y preceded by a consonant, change the y to i and add es (example: sky = skies).

5. For some nouns ending in f or fe, add s.  For others, change the f or fe to v and add es (example: gulf = gulfs; shelf = shelves).

6. For nouns ending in o preceded by a vowel, add s (example: radio = radios).

7. For nouns ending in o preceded by a consonant, add es (example: hero - heroes).

8. The plurals of a few nouns are formed in irregular ways (example: tooth = teeth; mouse = mice; ox = oxen; child = children).

9. For a few nouns, the singular and the plural forms are the same (example: deer; moose; Chinese).

10. For most compound nouns, form the plural of only the last word of the compound (example: blueprint = blueprints; two-year-old = two-year-olds).

11. For compound nouns in which one of the words is modified by the other word or words, form the plural of the noun modified (example: notary public = notaries public; attorney general = attorneys-general; rock garden = rock gardens).

12. For some nouns borrowed from other languages, the plural is formed as in the original language (example: alumnus = alumni)

  

CONCRETE AND ABSTRACT NOUNS:

In addition, nouns can be divided into concrete or abstract categories. 

A concrete noun names a person, place, or thing that can be perceived by one or more of the senses (sight, hearing, taste, touch, smell).  An abstract noun names an idea, a feeling, a quality, or a characteristic.

EXAMPLES:

Concrete – fire / garlic / cotton / horses / Liberty Bell

Abstract – self-confidence / strength / charm / ability / Zen

COUNTABLE AND  UNCOUNTABLE:

Furthermore, nouns can be considered either countable or uncountable.   Countable nouns can be quantified with numbers.  Uncountable nouns can not.

EXAMPLES:

Countable – car / desk / cup / house / bike

Uncountable – wood / air / milk / rain / happiness 

COMPOUND AND COLLECTIVE:

Nouns can also be categorized as compound and they can be categorized as collective.   

A compound noun consists of two or more words that together name a person, place, thing, or idea.  It may be written as one word, as separate words, or as a hyphenated word.

EXAMPLES:

Compound – sidewalk / attorney general / daughter-in-law

A collective noun is a singular noun that names a group.

EXAMPLES:

Collective – audience / bunch / fleet / jury / batch / herd / swarm / staff / bouquet / cluster

## Pronouns: 

#### **The pronoun is a word used in place of one or more nouns or pronouns.**

EXAMPLES:

Angelo borrowed a hammer and some nails.  He will return them tomorrow. 

[The pronoun he takes the place of the noun Angelo. The pronoun them takes the place of the nouns hammer and nails.]

Several of the students have entered the essay contest because they are extremely interested in the topic.

[The pronoun they takes the place of the pronoun Several.]

THE ANTECEDENT OF THE PRONOUN:

The word that a pronoun stands for is called the antecedent of the pronoun.  In the preceding examples, Angelo is the antecedent of He; hammer and nails are the antecedents of them; and Several is the antecedent of they.

PERSONAL PRONOUNS:

A personal pronoun refers to the one speaking (first person), the one spoken to (second person), or the one spoken about (third person).

EXAMPLES:

First Person – I, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours

Second Person – you, your, yours

Third Person – he, him, his, she, her, hers, it, its, they, them, their, theirs

REFLEXIVE AND INTENSIVE PRONOUNS:

A reflexive pronoun refers to the subject of a sentence and functions as the complement or as an object of a preposition.  An intensive pronoun emphasizes its antecedent.

EXAMPLES:

First Person – myself, ourselves

Second Person – yourself, yourselves

Third Person – himself, herself, itself, themselves

REFLEXIVE: Kimiko wrote a note to herself.

INTENSIVE: Leonora herself organized the school's recycling program.

DEMONSTRATIVE PRONOUNS:

A demonstrative pronoun points out a specific person, place, thing, or idea.

EXAMPLES: this, that, these, those

This is our favorite song by Ella Fitzgerald.

The apples I picked today taste better than these.

INTERROGATIVE PRONOUNS:

An interrogative pronoun introduces a question.

EXAMPLES: what, which, who, whom, whose

What is the answer to the last algebra problem?

Whose is this?

RELATIVE PRONOUNS:

A relative pronoun introduces an adjective clause.

EXAMPLES: that, which, who, whom, whose

The house that you saw is a historical landmark.

She is the woman who is running for mayor.

INDEFINITE PRONOUNS:

An indefinite pronoun refers to a person, a place, a thing, or an idea that may or may not be specifically named.

EXAMPLES: all, another, any, anybody, anyone, anything, both, each, each other, either, everybody, everyone, everything, few, many, more, most, much, neither, nobody, none, no one, nothing, one, one another, other, several, some, somebody, someone, something, such

I have packed everything we will need for the trip.

Has anyone seen my binoculars?**
